Machine-learning CFD surrogates are far faster than solvers, but their gains are reported
only against other learned models. Kernel interpolation over seven scalars parsed from
the AirfRANS case name---no network, no flow-field learning---scored through the
identical protocol,
gives \textbf{$88\%$ lower} volume-pressure error than a matched-budget Transolver
($8.4\times$), $62\%$ lower cross-flow error, and $4.9\times$/$3.8\times$ closer
agreement with the ground-truth field's own integrated lift and drag---not with the
official force labels. It loses on streamwise velocity ($6.5\times$) and eddy viscosity
($15\times$), hence $6.4\times$ on the four-channel training objective. The boundary is
sharp: at $128^2$, $92\%$ of its $u$ error and $90\%$ of its $v$ error lie inside $0.02$
chord of the wall, \textbf{$0.5\%$ of cells}, while \textbf{$98.7\%$ of the domain},
beyond $0.05$ chord, is reproduced at $R^2\ge0.9996$; at $512^2$ those shares are
$88\%$/$84\%$, a pre-registered \textsc{partial}. A surrogate buys the first cell and the
closure. The benchmark's primary metric cannot see that difference: a train-fit
regression on the case name ranks official lift at $0.9821$ $[0.9737,0.9866]$ and drag at
$0.9318$ $[0.8981,0.9531]$, above all four published lift entries---decisively for only
two ($3.8$ and $1.9$ seed standard deviations)---and above every drag entry, three of
four intervals spanning zero. The \texttt{reynolds} split does not test Reynolds
generalisation either---no test case has an in-range inlet velocity, yet a nondimensional
interpolator degrades $3\%$ where a dimensional one degrades $5.2$--$10.1\times$. The
surrogate-side physics residual ranks and certifies errors but cannot be descended: the
ground truth fails the check.
