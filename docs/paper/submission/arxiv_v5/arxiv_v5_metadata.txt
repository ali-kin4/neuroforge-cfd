WHAT CHANGED SINCE v3

1. THE PAPER WAS SPLIT. The calibrated trust layer and the
   force-coefficient covariate-null material are REMOVED; the residual
   audit is reduced to one subsection. The covariate null is the spine
   of a separate paper and will carry its own preprint under its own
   identifier; it must not be journal-published here. See
   docs/paper/sections/trust_layer_removed.tex and
   covariate_null_removed.tex.

2. THE MEASURE REVERSAL. One pair of predictions scored three ways in
   current use: 8.4x area-uniform, 0.44x node-weighted, 0.21x per node
   on the native cloud. Pre-registered rule D3 read the node-measure
   ratio as ARTIFACT and the manuscript withdraws the 8.4x in those
   words.

3. THE FAMILY BOUND, at n=200 (was n=3): the least-squares projection
   onto the span of all 800 transferred training fields is 25.0x
   Transolver's band error inside 0.005c, worse on all 200 cases paired
   (median 36.6x, above 10x on 185). The n=200 implementation was
   required to reproduce the frozen n=3 artifact at a worst relative
   difference of exactly 0.00e+00 (gate G7) before computing anything
   new.

4. THE HEADLINE REVERSAL, which is new in this version and is the
   reason for the title change. Re-posing the identical family in a
   parameter-free wall-following frame -- nothing retuned, both arms
   computed in one pass from one node array -- takes that bound from
   25x to 0.0044x (BODY-FITTED-BOUND-OPEN, pre-registered); paired per
   case it is below the surrogate on all 200 (median 0.0046, worst
   0.018). The physical arm rescored on the same restricted nodes
   reads 25.21x, so the restriction manufactured nothing. The BARRIER
   is an artifact of the coordinate system the baseline is posed in --
   not the gap: the deployed estimator in that frame improves 112x on
   the case-mean and still trails there (1.71x), with a paired median
   of 0.83x and 115/200 cases below the surrogate. The paper claims
   only the former.

5. THE CHANNEL SPLIT, read post hoc (no rule was registered for
   pressure). On PRESSURE there was never a barrier: the physical bound
   is already 0.00103x Transolver on matched nodes (974x better), and
   the wall-following frame makes it 1.58x worse on the case-mean,
   better on only 15/200 cases. Velocity varies steeply across a
   boundary layer and gains 5,665x; pressure is nearly constant across
   it and gains nothing. The near-wall result is a statement about u.

6. THE FRAME'S COST, measured: a wall-normal coordinate does not exist
   on 4.98% of near-wall nodes (4.19-6.75% across cases; 1.36% of the
   verdict band), at the trailing edge and the leading-edge stagnation
   point, where distinct points project onto a single surface vertex.
   Those nodes carry 99.99% of the frame's round-trip error. The
   exclusion criterion (amendment B-1) was derived from a failed gate
   and committed after a two-case smoke test while the 200-case run was
   executing; the rule and thresholds are those registered before any
   body-fitted run, and the manuscript states that provenance.

7. THE REPRESENTATION CEILING is restated correctly. The r128 raster's
   round-trip error at the native nodes exceeds the surrogate's by
   413x pooled and 495x near the wall. v3 and earlier drafts claimed
   this BOUNDS every published r128 field number; it does not, because
   a published metric scores a rasterised prediction against a
   rasterised truth and the representation error partly cancels. The
   claim is now blindness, plus a measured ranking INVERSION.

8. SURROGATE COMPETENCE, new: in the AirfRANS paper's own convention
   (standardised volume MSE, node-measure divisor) our Transolver is
   0.073e-2 on u and 0.099e-2 on p, against a best published 0.83e-2
   and 0.66e-2.

CLAIMS WITHDRAWN IN THIS VERSION, explicitly and in the text: the
8.4x area-uniform advantage as a finding; the near-wall
representational claim; and the assertion that the 413x bounds
published r128 numbers.
