WHY v5 EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
v3 was the manuscript as submitted to JCP, which desk-rejected it on
2026-09-07 for questionable originality (CMAME had done the same on
2026-08-02). Since then the work was reframed, then SPLIT, and then
its replacement headline was itself refuted by a pre-registered test.
The public record should carry the current version rather than a paper
that has been withdrawn twice over by its own authors.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE v3

1. THE PAPER WAS SPLIT. The calibrated trust layer and the
   force-coefficient/covariate-null material are REMOVED. The latter is
   the spine of a separate paper and will carry its own preprint under
   its own identifier; it must not be journal-published here. See
   docs/paper/sections/trust_layer_removed.tex and
   covariate_null_removed.tex.

2. THE MEASURE REVERSAL. One pair of predictions scored three ways in
   current use: 8.4x area-uniform, 0.44x node-weighted, 0.21x per node
   on the native cloud. Pre-registered rule D3 read the node-measure
   ratio as ARTIFACT and the manuscript withdraws the 8.4x in those
   words.

3. THE FAMILY BOUND, at n=200 (was n=3): the least-squares projection
   onto the span of all 800 transferred training fields is 25.0x
   Transolver's band error inside 0.005c. The n=200 implementation was
   required to reproduce the frozen n=3 artifact at a worst relative
   difference of exactly 0.00e+00 before computing anything new.

4. THE HEADLINE REVERSAL, which is new in this version and is the
   reason for the title change. Re-posing the identical family in a
   parameter-free wall-following frame -- nothing retuned, both arms
   computed in one pass from one node array -- takes that bound from
   25x to 0.0044x, better on 200 of 200 cases
   (BODY-FITTED-BOUND-OPEN, pre-registered). The physical arm rescored
   on the same restricted nodes still reads 25.2x, so the restriction
   manufactured nothing. The BARRIER is an artifact of the coordinate
   system the baseline is posed in -- not the gap: the deployed
   estimator in that frame improves 114x and still loses on the
   case-mean, and the paper claims only the former.

5. THE CHANNEL SPLIT, and it is the sharper test of the mechanism. On
   PRESSURE there was never a barrier: the physical-coordinate bound is
   already 0.00103x Transolver inside 0.005c, and the wall-following
   frame makes it 1.58x WORSE, better on only 15/200 cases. Velocity
   varies steeply across a boundary layer and gains 5,665x; pressure is
   nearly constant across it and gains nothing. The near-wall result is
   a VELOCITY statement.

6. THE FRAME'S COST, measured: a wall-normal coordinate does not exist
   on 4.98% of near-wall nodes (4.19-6.75%), at the trailing edge and
   the leading-edge stagnation region, where a fan of points projects
   onto a single vertex. Those nodes carry 99.99% of the frame's
   round-trip error.

7. THE REPRESENTATION CEILING is restated correctly. The r128 raster's
   round-trip error at the native nodes exceeds the surrogate's by
   413x pooled and 495x near the wall. v3 and earlier drafts claimed
   this BOUNDS every published r128 field number; it does not, because
   a published metric scores a rasterised prediction against a
   rasterised truth and the representation error partly cancels. The
   claim is now blindness, plus a measured ranking INVERSION.

8. SURROGATE COMPETENCE, new: in the AirfRANS paper's own convention
   (standardised volume MSE, node-measure divisor) our Transolver is
   0.073e-2 on u and 0.099e-2 on p, against a best published 0.83e-2
   and 0.66e-2.

CLAIMS WITHDRAWN IN THIS VERSION, explicitly and in the text: the
8.4x area-uniform advantage as a finding; the near-wall
representational claim; and the assertion that the 413x bounds
published r128 numbers.
