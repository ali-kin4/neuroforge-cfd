WHY v4 EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
v3 was the manuscript as submitted to JCP, which desk-rejected it on
2026-09-07 for questionable originality (CMAME had done the same on
2026-08-02). The paper was reframed rather than reformatted, and two new
experiments were run. The public record should carry the reframed version.

NEW MEASUREMENTS

1. Grid-refinement ladder for the residual floor. 24 AirfRANS cases at
   128^2 / 256^2 / 512^2, scored on a region fixed in physical units. The
   floor does NOT decay: 0.0624 -> 0.0779 -> 0.1067, rising in 21 of 24
   cases, fitted exponent p = -0.387. The decision rule was committed
   before the run (repository commit bf5106c).
   Controls: restoring the omitted turbulent-stress term moves the floor
   -0.01% to +0.18%; cubic instead of linear interpolation of the same
   reference cloud RAISES it 6-12%; a manufactured analytic solution
   through the same rasteriser gives a residual that falls.

2. Direct residual descent, which no earlier version of this work
   contained. Starting from the EXACT ground truth with no network in the
   loop, 300 steps on J = 0.5||R_h||^2 cut the residual 84% in 24/24 cases
   and take field error from zero to a median of 0.91.

CORRECTIONS TO CLAIMS MADE IN v3  -- read this before writing the comments
field, because one of them retracts something v3 announced as a result.

* v3's comments field says the no-slip-weight objection is closed "in
  closed form ... for every lambda >= 0. There is no crossover weight."
  **That is now known to be false at finer resolution.** The margin
  collapses monotonically with refinement (+9.3% at 128^2, +6.9% at 256^2,
  +3.9% at 362^2, -2.8% at 512^2) and the crossover weight where it flips
  is of order 10^2-10^3. The claim is restated in v4 as a statement at the
  deployed resolution. This retraction is the single most important reason
  v4 exists and it should be visible in the comments field.
* The acceptance gate's accuracy advantage does not survive an ungated
  fixed half-step control (95.8% of cases improved vs the gate's 89.3%).
  v4 claims the gate for its guarantee, not its accuracy.
* "AUROC ~ 0.9" is reattributed from the physics residual to the fused
  score; a physics-free ensemble score matches the residual on field error
  and a paired bootstrap cannot separate them. The physics wins outright
  only on drag (AUROC 0.952).
* The residual-floor theorem's leg (iii) advertised an upper bound where
  the thesis needs a lower one. Restated as a lower bound on the
  displacement of the minimiser.
* The third backbone (MeshGraphNet) is disclosed as evaluated off its
  training point density.

A full ledger of what was fixed, measured, conceded and refuted is in the
repository at docs/paper/review/RESOLUTION.md.

TITLE  (CHANGED -- paste this)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The ground truth fails its own physics check: what a surrogate-side RANS residual can and cannot certify

--------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT  (CHANGED -- paste this; plain text, no LaTeX)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Machine-learning surrogates for computational fluid dynamics predict steady
flow fields far faster than classical solvers, and the standard proposal for
trusting them is to have the surrogate check itself against the governing
equations. We show that this check rests on an operator the truth itself
cannot pass. On 200/200 real AirfRANS cases the monitored discrete
Reynolds-averaged residual of the ground-truth field is substantially
nonzero (mean 0.192), while the physically wrong uniform-freestream field
scores an exact zero -- the objective prefers a wrong field to the truth.
This is not a resolution artifact. The monitored operator is an inconsistent
discretisation, so its floor has a nonzero continuum limit; measured over 24
cases at 128^2 to 512^2 it does not merely persist but grows, rising in
21/24. Restoring the omitted closure term moves it under 0.2%, and a
higher-order interpolant raises it by 6-12%, so neither is what sets it. The
consequence for correction is stated in the form that holds universally:
started at the exact ground truth, with no network involved, gradient
descent cuts the residual by 84% while driving field error from zero to the
range a trained surrogate starts in, and the residual-selected iterate is
strictly worse than the error-optimal one in 24/24 cases -- a failure of
iterate selection, not of uniform harm, since from a perturbed start descent
often reduces error before overshooting. The boundary is the operator, not
the problem class: residual correction succeeds where the residual is the
solver's own.
